## Reviewed concepts

1. International dollar usage is multi-dimensional and should not be reduced to one reserves, payments, trade or FX statistic.
2. BIS currency-level FX turnover shares add to 200 percent because every transaction has two currency legs.
3. The dollar often operates as a vehicle currency in FX market routing; transaction currency and underlying economic exposure are not always the same.
4. Dollar invoicing can create exchange-rate exposure even when neither trade counterparty is based in the United States.
5. Structural international use of the dollar is a different variable and time horizon from the dollar’s current exchange-rate direction.

## Primary-source boundary

Allowed source domains for this batch are limited to:

- `federalreserve.gov` — Federal Reserve research on the international role of the dollar;
- `bis.org` — 2025 Triennial Central Bank Survey of FX turnover and measurement conventions;
- `imf.org` — research on invoicing currency in global trade.

The canonical card prose deliberately avoids freezing current reserve shares, current FX turnover levels or other dated usage statistics. The only percentage retained is the invariant BIS accounting convention that currency-level FX shares sum to 200 percent because two currencies are present in every FX transaction.

## Overlap review

The five concepts are narrower than existing cards:

- `card-usd` defines USD and its broad international roles but does not teach how to distinguish the measurement dimensions;
- `card-dxy-signal-system` distinguishes DXY from the dollar system but does not explain BIS turnover accounting or vehicle-currency structure;
- `card-dollar-move-not-proof` limits cross-asset inference from a dollar move but does not separate structural currency usage from exchange-rate direction;
- existing FX exposure cards cover transaction, translation and economic risk rather than the third-currency invoice convention itself.

## Access decision

All five cards remain `open`. They are foundational interpretation and measurement concepts rather than paid research outputs, live market signals or proprietary regime analysis.
