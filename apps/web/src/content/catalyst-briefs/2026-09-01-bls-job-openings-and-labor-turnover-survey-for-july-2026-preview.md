Confirmed facts

- Official timing: The BLS schedule and the JOLTS program page list the Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey for July 2026 to be released on Tuesday, 2026-09-01 at 10:00 a.m. Eastern Time (14:00 UTC). (See BLS schedule and JOLTS home pages.)

- Content: The JOLTS news release publishes national estimates of job openings, hires, separations (quits; layoffs and discharges; other separations) and related rates — the series traders and economists use to read labor demand and worker mobility.

- Market attention: major market calendars and preview services (for example, CME/Econoday and AP News previews) highlight JOLTS ahead of payrolls as a data point that may influence near‑term rate expectations and USD positioning.

Conditional interpretation (pre‑event, not a forecast)

- Directional mechanics: a stronger‑than‑expected print in job openings (and higher quits) tends to be interpreted as tighter labor demand and elevated worker bargaining power, which may support higher near‑term rate expectations and the USD. Conversely, a weaker print or rising layoffs may reduce near‑term rate repricing and weigh on the USD.

- Composition matters: markets focus not just on the headline openings number but on hires vs separations (net hiring), the quits rate (worker confidence/mobility), sector concentration (where openings are changing), and any revisions to prior months — these details drive nuanced market responses.

- Timing for reaction: the release at 10:00 a.m. ET (14:00 UTC) typically triggers immediate FX and rates moves; monitor Fed‑funds futures and the DXY in the first 30–120 minutes for initial price discovery, then equities and volatility markets for cross‑asset flows.

Quick pre‑event checklist (for traders/researchers)

- Confirm the official BLS release feed at 10:00 a.m. ET via the BLS JOLTS page or the BLS release calendar.
- Watch headline openings level and rate, hires and separations, quits rate, and notable sector changes.
- Note any upward/downward revisions to prior months’ JOLTS series.
- Compare JOLTS signals with consensus forecasts and positioning in Fed‑funds futures to gauge potential USD/rates sensitivity.
