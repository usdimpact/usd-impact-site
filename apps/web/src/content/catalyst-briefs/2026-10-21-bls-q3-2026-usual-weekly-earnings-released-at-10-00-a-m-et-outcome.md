## Key verified outcome (primary source)

- The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics released the Usual Weekly Earnings report for Q3 2026 on 2026-10-21. Median usual weekly earnings of full‑time wage and salary workers were $1,232 (not seasonally adjusted), up 1.5% year‑over‑year. Seasonally adjusted median was $1,239 (down from $1,258 the prior quarter). The release notes a 3.5% 12‑month rise in the CPI‑U over the same period. (Sources: BLS news release and tables.)

## Conditional cross‑asset transmission (separate from verified facts)

- Real incomes / consumption: With nominal wages (+1.5% YoY) below CPI (+3.5% YoY), real weekly earnings are negative on a 12‑month basis. If this persists, consumer spending could soften, which tends to be a later‑look negative for cyclical equity sectors.

- Monetary‑policy expectations → rates → USD: Wage surprises (either direction) can shift short‑term Fed expectations. Weaker wage momentum tends to reduce near‑term upside to Fed hike odds (pressuring short‑end yields and the dollar); stronger wages can support higher yields and a firmer dollar.

- Inflation narratives and term premia: Wage strength is a core input to inflation expectations. Persistent wage acceleration may lift inflation risk premia and steepen yields; the reverse can compress term premia.

- Risk sentiment and equities: Upside wage surprises may trigger equity rotation away from rate‑sensitive growth names, while downside surprises can lift risk appetite if interpreted as easing of monetary pressure.

## What to watch next (concrete triggers and metrics)

- Intraday moves in 2y and 10y Treasury yields immediately after the release and in the following 24–48 hours.
- DXY and major‑pair USD behavior vs. yield moves (confirm whether FX follows rates or risk flows). 
- Fed‑funds futures shifts and commentary from Fed officials for signs of repricing. 
- Equity sector leadership in the S&P 500 (cyclicals vs defensives; growth vs value rotation). 
- Upcoming inflation and labor prints (next CPI, and subsequent labor market releases) which will determine whether wages are signaling a trend.
