## Executive view

The Federal Open Market Committee held the federal funds target range at **3.75%–4.00%** on October 28, 2026. The statement was approved by a **10–2 vote**. Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack dissented because they preferred to raise the target range by **25 basis points**.

The operating settings in the implementation note are unchanged from September: IORB **3.90%**, standing overnight repo **4.0%**, ON RRP **3.75%** with a **$160 billion daily per-counterparty limit**, and primary credit **4.0%**. The October settings take effect October 29.

These are verified Federal Reserve facts. This brief does not add a market-reaction claim, label the decision hawkish or dovish, or recalculate Three Dials v2 intraday.

## Official decision and vote

The Committee maintained the target range at **3.75%–4.00%**. The September meeting had approved its action unanimously; the October action passed **10–2**.

The two dissents were in one direction: Cook and Hammack preferred a **25 bp increase**. That records a difference over the adopted stance, but it is not a forecast of the next decision.

## Implementation note

The Federal Reserve left the major operating rates and limits unchanged:

- interest on reserve balances: **3.90%**;
- standing overnight repo: **4.0%**;
- overnight reverse repo: **3.75%**;
- ON RRP per-counterparty limit: **$160 billion per day**;
- primary credit: **4.0%**.

The Desk directive continues to include Treasury-bill purchases and, if needed, other Treasury securities with remaining maturities of three years or less to maintain ample reserves, Treasury rollovers, and reinvestment of agency principal into Treasury bills.

Those instructions describe policy implementation. They do not show actual repo usage, funding stress, reserve scarcity or a measured change in liquidity.

## Statement changes versus September

The October statement describes economic activity as expanding at a **strong pace**. September had used **solid pace**.

Inflation remains described as **elevated**. The October statement adds that accumulating more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 2% goal.

This comparison records wording changes in official statements. It does not convert those words into a guaranteed future rate path.

## Press-conference boundary

Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference is a separate source from the written policy decision. Any material remarks should be added only after they can be verified from an official Federal Reserve video or transcript.

Reporter questions, live commentary and secondary summaries are not substitutes for the statement or implementation note.

## Why it matters

The outcome separates several questions that are often combined incorrectly:

1. the **target range** did not change;
2. the **vote composition** changed;
3. the **implementation settings** did not change;
4. some **statement language** changed;
5. observed market reaction remains a separate evidence problem.

For USD Impact, the clean sequence is therefore to verify later changes in USD direction, real rates and liquidity/stress before attributing movements in gold, equities, Bitcoin, EURUSD or WTI to the Fed decision.

## Common mistake

Do not treat the two dissents as a promise of a future hike, unchanged implementation settings as evidence of unchanged market liquidity, or a first asset-price move as proof of the policy signal.

A policy decision, implementation directive, press conference and market reaction are different evidence layers.
