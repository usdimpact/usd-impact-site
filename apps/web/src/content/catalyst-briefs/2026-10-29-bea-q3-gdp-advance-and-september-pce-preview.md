## Executive pre-event brief

### What it is

The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis is scheduled to publish two major releases at **8:30 a.m. EDT on October 29, 2026**:

1. the **advance estimate of third-quarter 2026 GDP**; and
2. **Personal Income and Outlays for September 2026**, including the headline and core PCE price indexes.

The two releases arrive together, so the growth, inflation and household-income evidence should be read as one information window rather than as isolated headlines.

### Why it matters

The October 28 FOMC decision is already fixed: the Committee held the federal funds target range at 3.75%–4.00%. The October 29 releases add new information to the growth and inflation sides of the policy outlook.

For USD Impact, the useful sequence is:

1. verify the official GDP and PCE figures;
2. separate growth from inflation and revisions;
3. check whether rates and the dollar move consistently with the data mix;
4. then assess gold, equities and Bitcoin conditionally.

This preview does not use a consensus forecast and does not state an outcome before BEA publishes it.

### Confirmed official baseline

**GDP:** BEA's latest completed estimate says real GDP increased at an annual rate of **2.2% in Q2 2026**. The October 29 release will be the first, or advance, estimate for Q3.

**PCE inflation:** In August, the headline PCE price index rose **0.3% month over month** and **3.4% year over year**. Core PCE, excluding food and energy, rose **0.2% month over month** and **3.0% year over year**.

**Household data:** August personal income increased **0.2%**, current-dollar PCE increased **0.9%**, real PCE increased **0.6%**, and the personal saving rate was **4.1%**.

These are historical baselines, not forecasts for September or Q3.

### What moves it

**GDP composition:** The headline annualized growth rate matters, but so do consumption, business investment, inventories, government spending and net exports. A strong headline driven by volatile components can carry a different policy implication from broad domestic-demand strength.

**PCE inflation:** Headline and core monthly inflation provide the freshest BEA inflation readings. Year-over-year rates add context, but monthly changes and revisions can matter for the near-term inflation path.

**Income and spending:** Personal income, disposable income, real PCE and saving can help show whether household demand is being supported by income growth or by a lower saving rate.

**Revisions:** BEA can revise prior estimates. A current print should not be interpreted without checking whether August or prior GDP history also changed.

### Common mistake

Do not treat the GDP advance estimate as final. It is the first estimate for Q3 and can be revised.

Do not combine the GDP price measures and monthly PCE price indexes as if they were the same statistic.

Do not assume that stronger growth automatically means a stronger dollar, or that softer inflation automatically means a weaker dollar. Cross-asset reaction depends on the mix of surprises, prior pricing and the rates response.

### What to watch in practice

1. Q3 real GDP annualized growth and its contribution mix.
2. September headline PCE: month over month and year over year.
3. September core PCE: month over month and year over year.
4. September personal income, disposable personal income, real PCE and saving rate.
5. Revisions to August Personal Income and Outlays and any relevant GDP history.
6. After the release, verify USD direction and real-rate pressure before attributing moves in gold, equities or Bitcoin to the data.
7. Keep the normal Three Dials v2 methodology unchanged; no intraday DXY or other proxy should replace the approved Broad USD, H.15 real-yield or SOFR-minus-IORB inputs.

## Key takeaway

October 29 combines the first official estimate of Q3 growth with September PCE inflation and household-income/spending data at the same 8:30 a.m. EDT release time. The safest interpretation is to verify the full BEA data mix first, keep revisions visible, and then test the transmission through rates and the dollar rather than pre-assigning a market direction.
