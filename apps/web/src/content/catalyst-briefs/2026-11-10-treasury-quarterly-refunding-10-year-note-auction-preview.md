## Pre‑event brief — Treasury 10‑year note auction (2026‑11‑10)

Key official facts

- Treasury’s Quarterly Refunding statement (Nov 4, 2026) schedules a $42 billion 10‑year note (maturing Nov 15, 2036) to be auctioned at 1:00 p.m. ET on Tuesday, November 10, 2026; settlement is scheduled for Monday, November 16, 2026. (Treasury Quarterly Refunding Statement.)
- The broader refunding package equals $125 billion (3‑yr $58bn; 10‑yr $42bn; 30‑yr $25bn) and Treasury states these auctions will take place on a yield basis. (Treasury Quarterly Refunding Statement.)
- Auction timing, sizes and supporting auction/buyback documents were published in the Quarterly Refunding documentation and the Treasury tentative auction schedule. (Treasury Quarterly Refunding documents and Tentative Auction Schedule.)

Why this matters (concise)

- A $42bn on‑the‑run 10‑year auction is a core benchmark event. The stop‑out yield, bid‑to‑cover, and indirect bidder participation are closely watched signals of demand for U.S. duration. Weaker demand or higher stop‑out yields could push 10y yields higher and prompt USD strength through rates channels; stronger demand could help compress term premia and relieve short‑term upward pressure on yields.

Conditional event dynamics (what tends to happen)

- If the auction prints with a materially higher stop‑out yield than the pre‑auction market, expect a near‑term uptick in 10y yields, potential widening in swap spreads, and transient USD buying into the initial re‑pricing window.
- If indirect bidding (foreign/private) is robust and bid‑to‑cover is healthy, the auction may be absorbed with limited secondary market disruption — this tends to calm immediate risk‑asset pressures.

Market signals to monitor (on the day)

- Official Treasury auction result (stop‑out yield, bid‑to‑cover, indirect %): primary market signal for demand.
- Pre‑ and post‑auction movement in the on‑the‑run 10y cash and futures basis (10y futures and USGG10YR on major screens).
- Changes in repo and overnight funding spreads and dealer inventories (if observable) — indicate immediate liquidity impact.
- Any contemporaneous Treasury buyback or large TGA flow disclosures that alter net supply absorption.
