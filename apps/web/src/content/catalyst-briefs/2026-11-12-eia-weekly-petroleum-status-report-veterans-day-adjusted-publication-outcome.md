## Executive view

The U.S. Energy Information Administration published the **Weekly Petroleum Status Report on Thursday, 12 November 2026**, covering the **week ending Friday, 6 November**. The issuer's release-specific [historical archive](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/archive/2026/2026_11_12/wpsr_2026_11_12.php) explicitly distinguishes the government release date from the data week.

Commercial crude oil inventories excluding the Strategic Petroleum Reserve rose **4.7 million barrels** to **414.4 million**. Gasoline stocks rose **1.8 million barrels** and distillate stocks rose **4.1 million**. Those figures are EIA inventory observations, **not evidence of a particular oil-price or dollar reaction**.

## What the original WPSR documents establish

The release-specific [EIA summary](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/archive/2026/2026_11_12/download/summary.txt) and [Table 1 CSV](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/archive/2026/2026_11_12/download/table1.csv) independently identify the same published inventory changes after rounding.

| Petroleum measure | Prior week, million barrels | Week ending 6 Nov, million barrels | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Commercial crude excluding SPR | 409.760 | 414.417 | +4.657 (about +4.7) |
| Total motor gasoline | 208.958 | 210.757 | +1.799 (about +1.8) |
| Distillate fuel oil | 99.259 | 103.330 | +4.071 (about +4.1) |

The EIA summary also reports crude inputs at approximately **17.0 million barrels per day**, up **322,000 barrels per day**, and refinery utilization at **95.2%**. These are reported estimates. Inventory levels and changes are rounded for public summaries; totals and components may not add exactly because of independent rounding.

## How this may transmit to WTI, Brent and the dollar

An increase in commercial crude inventories can be relevant to near-term balances, while product stocks and refinery utilization provide context for demand and supply. An observed market move would require information **not supplied by these inventory documents**: actual price quotes, market expectations, positioning and other simultaneous news.

Likewise, a sustained oil-price move could affect inflation expectations and rates, and rates may sometimes affect the U.S. dollar. This is a **conditional transmission framework**, not an assertion that the 12 November WPSR caused a particular WTI, Brent, DXY or Treasury-yield change.
