## What this framework is

The Dollar Framework is an educational structure for thinking about how the macro environment shapes cross-asset relationships. It is not a forecasting model, a trading system, or a guarantee of any outcome. Asset relationships are regime-dependent — they shift over time as policy, geopolitics, and market structure change.

## Dial 1 — Dollar direction

The DXY measures the dollar against six major currencies, weighted approximately 57% toward the Euro. For a fuller picture, watch the Federal Reserve's Broad Dollar Index alongside DXY. A strengthening dollar historically tends to tighten global financial conditions — raising the cost of dollar-denominated debt, pressuring commodity prices, and reducing risk appetite in emerging markets.

## Dial 2 — Real rates

Real interest rates — the nominal rate minus inflation expectations — are the second dial. Rising real rates have historically been associated with dollar strength and gold weakness. The 10-year U.S. TIPS yield on FRED is a standard proxy.

**Common mistake:** Confusing nominal rates with real rates. A high nominal yield during high inflation may represent a low or negative real yield. What matters for many asset relationships is the inflation-adjusted rate.

## Dial 3 — Liquidity and stress

Global dollar liquidity is the third dial. When it tightens, credit spreads widen, risk appetite falls, and assets that depend on cheap funding come under pressure. Key signals include the Fed balance sheet, Fed funds rate path, credit spreads, and VIX.

## Dial 4 — Broad USD confirmation

Check dollar strength against a broader set of currencies — particularly emerging-market and commodity-linked FX. If DXY is rising but the Broad Dollar Index is not confirming, the move may be currency-specific rather than systemic.

## The transmission chain

Fed Policy → U.S. Rates → Dollar Direction → Global Liquidity → Risk Appetite → Asset Prices

This chain is not linear and not guaranteed. It is an educational framework for thinking through how macro forces have historically connected — not a predictive formula.

## Key takeaway

Reading dollar regimes means tracking all four dials, not just the headline index. Relationships between these variables and asset prices are historically observable but regime-dependent — they do not operate on a fixed schedule and can break down.
