## Executive synthesis

The September 21–October 16 reporting window combined labor and demand evidence, dated policy and credit context, and September inflation releases. Across its four completed Fridays, the USD Impact Score remained in a soft-dollar regime. The final published reading was less negative than the opening one, while the classification stayed unchanged.

The stable classification contained some variation in component contributions. Gold and the S&P 500 remained the largest negative contributors. Treasury yields and DXY supplied positive offsets in every source week, while the final snapshot added a positive VIX contribution and placed WTI among the three largest absolute contributions.

## The score path and its comparison basis

The originally published readings were **−0.56 on [September 25](/reports/weekly/2026-09-25)**, **−0.50 on [October 2](/reports/weekly/2026-10-02)**, **−0.51 on [October 9](/reports/weekly/2026-10-09)** and **−0.47 on [October 16](/reports/weekly/2026-10-16)**. All four remained below the −0.30 boundary shown in their source briefs.

The difference between the first and last published values was approximately **+0.09 score points**. Those four Friday observations span **three weekly intervals** and retain their different publication vintages. By comparison, the October 16 brief's **+0.18 four-week change** compares observations four weeks apart within the history recalculated for that particular snapshot. The date interval and the data vintage both differ; the two changes answer different questions.

This report preserves the source briefs' original scores. It does not recalculate earlier values using the October 16 snapshot. The path describes published regime measurements, not asset returns or investment performance.

## Persistent contributors and a change in breadth

In the first three source weeks, gold, the S&P 500 and the 10-year Treasury component occupied the three largest absolute contribution positions. The first two contributed on the softer-dollar side; the Treasury component provided a firmer-dollar offset.

The [October 16 brief](/reports/weekly/2026-10-16) listed **SPX −0.300, GOLD −0.291 and WTI −0.244** as its three largest absolute contributions. The 10-year and 2-year Treasury components and DXY remained positive. VIX, which had contributed negatively in the first three published snapshots, also contributed positively in the final one. The change in the sign of that contribution did not produce a regime transition.

These are contributions to the standardized score at each publication vintage. They do not establish the direction or size of a same-day market response to an individual economic release.

## Labor and demand across the source weeks

The [September 25 brief](/reports/weekly/2026-09-25) recorded initial claims of 197,000 for the week ended September 19 alongside structural labor evidence, including median employee tenure of 4.1 years in January 2026. The [October 2 brief](/reports/weekly/2026-10-02) then recorded September nonfarm payroll growth of 29,000 and unemployment of 4.2%.

That October 2 source also carried August real consumer spending growth of 0.6% over the month and second-quarter real GDP growth revised to a 2.2% annual rate. The [October 9 brief](/reports/weekly/2026-10-09) carried forward the same payroll release while adding context from August manufacturing orders, up 0.1%, inventories, up 0.5%, and a larger August trade deficit as imports and exports rose. Repeated coverage of the payroll release is one observation, not a second employment result.

## Inflation, energy and purchasing power

The [October 2 source](/reports/weekly/2026-10-02) recorded August headline PCE inflation of 3.4% year over year and core PCE inflation of 3.0%. The [October 16 source](/reports/weekly/2026-10-16) added the later September consumer, producer and trade-price releases. These measure different baskets and reference periods; their levels should not be read as a single continuous inflation series.

September CPI rose 0.6% monthly and 3.7% annually, with gasoline an important driver, while core CPI rose 0.3% monthly and real average hourly earnings fell 0.5%. September final-demand PPI rose 1.3% monthly and 6.3% annually, including a 10.8% monthly increase in energy PPI. The CPI and PPI monthly price changes were seasonally adjusted; their annual changes were unadjusted.

The October 16 Daily update carried into the final Weekly added import prices up 1.1% monthly and 8.1% annually, and export prices up 2.2% monthly and 11.6% annually. Those trade-price changes were not seasonally adjusted. Together, the releases broadened the inflation evidence available by the end of the window without establishing a verified same-day currency, real-yield or asset-price reaction.

## Dated policy, credit and household context

The [September 25 source](/reports/weekly/2026-09-25) treated the September 16 Fed increase to 3.75%–4.00% as the existing policy backdrop. That decision preceded this report's September 21 start. Its financing evidence included broadly unchanged dealer-financing terms and reserve balances averaging $2.930 trillion in the week ended September 23.

The [October 2 source](/reports/weekly/2026-10-02) carried bank-credit observations for the week ended September 16. The [October 9 source](/reports/weekly/2026-10-09) distinguished dated currency observations from their release date and kept nominal and real Treasury yields separate. The [final source week](/reports/weekly/2026-10-16) used the Federal Reserve's October 9 H.8 release, with observations through September 30, and its 2025 household finance survey.

Those reference periods are part of the evidence. Aggregating the briefs into a Monthly does not turn older bank or survey observations into current market prices, or supply market reactions that the source editions did not verify.

## Methodology note

This monthly report adds no new external event claims. It synthesizes only the four published Weekly USD Impact Briefs for the consecutive completed Fridays of September 25, October 2, October 9 and October 16, 2026. The reporting window starts with the first source brief's September 21 period start and ends on October 16. Each theme identifies the source Weeklies supporting it.

The score path copies each source brief's canonical `score.value` and regime exactly. It retains each originally published data vintage rather than restating the path on a common recalculation date. Weekly and four-week changes within an individual brief come from the history recalculated when that brief's immutable score snapshot was produced; they need not equal subtraction between the originally published values shown here. Rounded numbers in the prose are for readability; the underlying score path retains full precision.

The source Weeklies draw on published Daily editions and archived deterministic Score snapshots. Economic claims here remain bounded by those published reports, with release dates, observation periods and the limits of verified market evidence preserved.
