## Executive view

The dominant transmission risk is an energy shock moving from physical supply routes into inflation expectations, Treasury yields, the dollar, and financial conditions. The relationship is not mechanical: gold can rise alongside the dollar during acute geopolitical stress, while oil can ignore a stronger dollar when physical disruption risk dominates.

## Cross-asset transmission

The practical sequence to monitor is:

**Energy-route risk → oil and gas risk premium → inflation expectations → nominal yields and policy pricing → DXY and liquidity conditions → gold, Bitcoin, and equity valuations.**

The sequence can break at any stage. Inventory data may challenge the physical-tightness narrative; central banks may distinguish temporary energy inflation from persistent underlying inflation; and company earnings may offset macro pressure with stronger cash generation.

## Risk controls for interpretation

- Treat intraday prices as time-stamped observations, not permanent levels.
- Separate verified events from scenario analysis.
- Distinguish WTI, Henry Hub, TTF, and LNG rather than treating “energy” as one market.
- Avoid assuming that gold and Bitcoin respond identically to geopolitical stress.
- Read Magnificent Seven earnings through revenue quality, margins, capital expenditure, and free cash flow—not headline growth alone.
