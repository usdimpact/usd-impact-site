Executive view

- Market backdrop: Recent verified developments center on the Federal Reserve’s July 28–29 policy calendar, EIA’s revised global oil-supply and price outlook, and NVIDIA’s July 21 AI-infrastructure announcements. Together, these developments frame near-term USD, rates, energy and equity risk without determining a single directional outcome.

Key drivers (verified facts)
- Federal Reserve calendar: The Fed’s July calendar schedules H.6 Money Stock Measures for July 28, followed by the FOMC decision and press conference on July 29. This creates a concentrated window for policy and liquidity-sensitive repricing. (sources: fed-fomc-calendar, fed-recent-postings)
- Energy supply and price outlook: EIA’s July STEO raised global oil-production expectations and forecast lower crude oil and gasoline prices as shipping through the Strait of Hormuz recovered. The July 22 Weekly Petroleum Status Report covers the week ending July 17, and the next release is scheduled for July 29. (sources: eia-steo, eia-wpsr)
- Technology event flow: NVIDIA’s July 21 newsroom updates covered Vera Rubin systems, Spectrum-6 networking and U.S. manufacturing capacity for AI infrastructure. (source: nvidia-newsroom)

Conditional cross-asset interpretation
- A more restrictive-than-expected FOMC message could support the USD and U.S. yields, while a less restrictive message could weaken rate support for the dollar. Risk sentiment and positioning may alter either reaction.
- Lower energy prices can ease headline inflation pressure. The USD effect is not automatic because lower inflation may reduce rate support, while cheaper energy can support growth and risk appetite.
- NVIDIA infrastructure announcements can support sentiment toward AI-exposed equities. Any USD effect is indirect and depends on whether risk appetite or U.S. growth and rate expectations dominate.

Risks to the view
- An unexpected change in FOMC guidance or tone at the July 29 press conference could produce a sharp USD and rates reaction.
- Renewed energy-supply disruption could reverse EIA’s lower-price path and restore inflation pressure.
- Liquidity data or broader funding-market developments could amplify rate moves independently of the policy decision.

Watchlist
- July 28: H.6 Money Stock Measures and the start of the two-day FOMC meeting. (source: fed-fomc-calendar)
- July 29: FOMC policy decision and press conference. (source: fed-fomc-calendar)
- July 29: EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report. (source: eia-wpsr)
