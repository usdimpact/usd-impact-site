Executive view
- Verified decision: The FOMC kept the federal funds target range at 3.50%–3.75% by a 9–3 vote. Three members preferred a 25 bp increase. (fomc-statement-2026-07-29)
- Verified assessment: The Committee described activity as solid and the labor market as stable, while saying inflation remained above its 2% goal and was being affected partly by supply shocks, including energy. (fomc-statement-2026-07-29)
- Verified implementation: Administered and standing-facility rates were unchanged, and the Fed retained an ample-reserves operating framework. (fomc-implementation-note-2026-07-29)

Key drivers for USD and rates
- Hawkish distribution inside a hold: The unchanged target range avoids an immediate tightening step, but three votes for a hike show that renewed tightening remains part of the policy debate. Strong wage, demand, or inflation data could therefore lift front-end yields and support the dollar; softer data could reduce that pressure.
- Resilient growth versus elevated inflation: The statement’s combination of solid activity and above-target inflation leaves policy sensitive to whether supply-driven price increases spread into wages and services. The July 31 Employment Cost Index is the nearest confirmed test of that channel.
- Operating liquidity: The unchanged interest-on-reserves, repo, reverse-repo, and discount-rate settings anchor overnight money markets. The July 30 H.4.1 release may provide additional context on reserve balances, the Treasury General Account, and facility usage.
- Communication regime: Chair Warsh emphasized a firm 2% inflation target and less reliance on forward guidance. More data-led pricing can raise volatility around each major release even when the policy rate itself is unchanged. (warsh-opening-statement-2026-07-29)

Risks to the base interpretation
- A stronger-than-expected wage or labor-demand signal could reinforce the hawkish dissenters’ case and increase upward pressure on short-dated yields and the USD.
- Softer labor-cost or openings data could strengthen expectations that the unchanged rate is sufficiently restrictive, potentially easing yields and the dollar.
- Energy or geopolitical supply shocks could keep inflation risk elevated even if domestic demand indicators moderate.
- Unexpected reserve or money-market changes could affect short-term liquidity without implying a change in the FOMC’s target range.
