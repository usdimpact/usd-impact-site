Executive view
- The Employment Cost Index increased 0.9% in the second quarter, with wages and salaries up 0.9% and benefits up 1.0%. (bls-eci-june-2026)
- Civilian compensation costs rose 3.4% over the year, while wages increased 3.2% and benefits increased 3.8%. (bls-eci-june-2026)
- The release follows a 9–3 Federal Reserve decision to hold the target range at 3.50%–3.75%, with three members preferring a 25 bp increase. (fomc-statement-2026-07-29)

Key drivers for USD and rates
- Labor-cost persistence: The quarterly ECI reading keeps compensation pressure relevant for the inflation outlook. If later data show firm demand and services inflation, front-end yields and the dollar may remain supported.
- Gradual annual moderation: The 3.4% annual compensation pace is below earlier cycle peaks but remains material for a central bank seeking sustained progress toward 2% inflation.
- Policy distribution: Three votes for a rate increase show that the debate includes renewed tightening, not only the timing of eventual easing.
- Liquidity implementation: Administered rates remain aligned with the unchanged policy range, leaving reserve balances and facility usage as separate short-term liquidity signals.

Confirmed watchlist
- August 4, 10:00 a.m. ET: JOLTS for June 2026. (bls-august-2026-schedule)
- August 6, 8:30 a.m. ET: preliminary Productivity and Costs for the second quarter of 2026. (bls-august-2026-schedule)

Risks to the base interpretation
- Softer labor-demand data could offset the firm ECI signal and reduce upward pressure on yields and the USD.
- Strong productivity could absorb compensation growth without equivalent unit-labor-cost pressure.
- Energy and other supply shocks could keep inflation elevated independently of domestic wage trends.
- Cross-asset reactions depend on expectations and positioning, not only the direction of a single data release.
