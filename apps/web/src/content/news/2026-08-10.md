Executive view

The coming week centers on U.S. CPI (Aug 12) and a cluster of Treasury liquidity operations in the Aug 10–12 window. The Fed’s July 29 statement reaffirmed a conditional pause in the target range (3.50–3.75%) while noting inflation remains above target — a backdrop that makes the mid-week CPI print and Treasury buybacks the primary near-term drivers of DXY, U.S. rates, and risk appetite.

Key drivers
- Fed communications: July 29 FOMC statement keeps conditional pause but leaves upside risk from inflation. (Source: Federal Reserve).
- Near-term inflation calendar: CPI (BLS) Aug 12 is the immediate market mover; PPI (Aug 13) is a follow-up that can provide supply-side context after CPI. (Source: BLS).
- Treasury liquidity and supply: Tentative Treasury buyback/cash-management operations in the Aug 10–12 window and the ongoing refunding process can influence short-term liquidity, dealer positioning, and bill/note yields—second-order drivers for USD and risk assets. (Source: Treasury).

Risks and conditional scenarios
- If CPI prints materially above consensus on Aug 12, market-implied Fed tightening probability and short-term Treasury yields could rise, supporting the USD and pressuring equities.
- If CPI is softer than expected, front-end yields could ease and risk assets might rally, weighing on USD.
- Large or sizable Treasury cash-management operations could tighten or loosen liquidity, amplifying moves in short-term rates and term premia.

Watchlist (next 7 days)
- Aug 12: BLS CPI for July (high priority).
- Aug 11–12: Treasury buyback/cash-management operations (monitor announced sizes and eligible CUSIPs).
- Aug 13: BLS PPI release (follow-through inflation signal).
