Executive view

July inflation moderated at the margin without producing a large initial market repricing. Headline CPI rose 0.1% in July and 3.4% over 12 months, while core CPI rose 0.2% and 2.5%. The dollar slipped and Treasury yields moved only modestly after the in-line release. That combination is consistent with a data-dependent regime rather than a decisive shift in the policy outlook.

Key drivers

- Inflation: Annual headline CPI eased from 3.5% to 3.4%, and annual core CPI eased from 2.6% to 2.5%.
- Composition: Shelter rose 0.1% and accounted for roughly two-thirds of the monthly all-items increase. Energy fell 1.5%, including a 2.9% decline in gasoline.
- Market reaction: The dollar weakened modestly after the report, while the 2-year yield dipped and the 10-year yield moved slightly higher.
- Policy backdrop: The FOMC held rates at 3.50–3.75% on July 29, with three members preferring a 25-basis-point increase.

Conditional cross-asset interpretation

- U.S. rates and DXY: Further evidence of moderating core inflation may reduce pressure for a more restrictive policy path, which could weigh on front-end yields and the dollar. A firmer PPI or renewed energy pressure could reverse that interpretation.
- EURUSD, gold, and Bitcoin: A softer dollar and contained real-rate response may be supportive, but the limited initial move argues for caution about extrapolating a durable trend from one release.
- U.S. equities: In-line inflation can reduce immediate discount-rate pressure on long-duration equities. The effect may fade if producer prices or FOMC minutes revive tightening concerns.
- Energy: July’s monthly energy decline reduced headline CPI, but the energy index remained 14.7% higher over 12 months. Future oil and fuel moves remain an important inflation transmission channel.

Next seven days

- Aug 13: July PPI and the Federal Reserve H.4.1 reserve-balances release.
- Aug 18: July U.S. import and export price indexes.
- Aug 19: FOMC minutes for the July 28–29 meeting.

Risks and caveats

- One CPI report does not establish a durable inflation trend.
- The split between softer energy and still-elevated annual inflation can produce different signals across nominal yields, real yields, and the dollar.
- Early market reactions can reverse as liquidity, positioning, and subsequent data alter the policy interpretation.
