## Executive view

The dominant cross-asset signal is mixed. July headline producer prices were unchanged as energy and goods prices fell, but the core measure excluding food, energy, and trade services rose 0.4%. At the same time, a large weekly U.S. crude inventory build softened the immediate oil impulse. Treasury duration supply remains today's main liquidity test through the 30-year auction.

## What changed

- BLS reported that final-demand producer prices were unchanged in July and 4.7% higher over 12 months. The measure excluding food, energy, and trade services rose 0.4% on the month and 4.7% over 12 months.
- EIA reported a 17.4 million-barrel increase in commercial crude inventories excluding the SPR for the week ended August 7. Its cited WTI spot price was $79.77 on August 7, $6.39 below the prior week.
- Treasury scheduled a $25 billion 30-year bond auction for August 13, with the August refunding auctions settling on August 17.

## Cross-asset transmission

The producer-price mix may keep rate expectations sensitive to the distinction between headline energy disinflation and firmer underlying services. The crude inventory build may reduce near-term oil-led inflation pressure if later data confirm softer balances. Treasury auction demand remains an independent rates-and-liquidity channel: a larger yield concession could support DXY and pressure real-rate-sensitive assets, while firm demand could limit that transmission.

## What to watch

- The August 13 30-year Treasury auction and the curve's response after the result.
- Whether the large crude inventory increase persists or reverses in the next EIA report.
- Whether future inflation releases confirm the gap between softer headline energy components and firmer underlying producer-price measures.
- Treasury auction settlement on August 17 and the scheduled buyback operations on August 18 and August 20.

## Key takeaway

The August 13 setup is not a single-direction dollar signal. Softer headline producer prices and a large crude build reduce one inflation channel, while the firmer core PPI measure and long-duration Treasury supply keep rates, DXY, gold, and growth equities sensitive to real-yield and liquidity repricing.
