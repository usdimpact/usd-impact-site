## Executive view

The current signal is mixed rather than directional. July headline producer prices were unchanged as goods and energy prices fell, but the core measure excluding food, energy, and trade services rose 0.4%. Federal Reserve data simultaneously showed a higher Treasury cash balance and lower reserve balances, creating a tighter weekly liquidity input ahead of the August refunding settlement.

## What changed

- BLS reported that final-demand producer prices were unchanged in July and 4.7% higher over 12 months. The measure excluding food, energy, and trade services rose 0.4% on the month and 4.7% over 12 months. (bls-ppi-july-2026)
- The Federal Reserve reported average reserve balances of $2.944 trillion for the week ended August 12, down $49.290 billion from the prior week. The Treasury General Account increased by $56.626 billion to $963.950 billion. (fed-h41-2026-08-13)
- Total Federal Reserve assets increased by $11.388 billion to $6.760 trillion, partially offsetting the tighter weekly liquidity signal from Treasury cash accumulation. (fed-h41-2026-08-13)
- Treasury's $125 billion August refunding package settles on August 17 and is expected to raise approximately $28.7 billion of new cash. (treasury-august-2026-refunding)

## Cross-asset transmission

The producer-price split may keep rate expectations sensitive to the difference between weaker energy components and firmer underlying services. The higher Treasury cash balance and lower reserve balances can pressure near-term liquidity, although the modest increase in Federal Reserve assets makes the net signal mixed. The transmission into DXY, gold, Bitcoin, and equities depends on how Treasury yields, funding conditions, and policy expectations respond.

## What to watch

- Treasury refunding settlement on August 17 and any effect on funding conditions or the yield curve.
- Industrial production and capacity utilization on August 18.
- FOMC minutes and the next EIA petroleum report on August 19.
- Whether the next H.4.1 release confirms or reverses the decline in reserve balances.

## Key takeaway

The August 14 setup does not justify a simple bullish or bearish dollar conclusion. Headline producer inflation softened, core producer inflation remained firm, and weekly liquidity data tightened through a higher Treasury cash balance and lower bank reserves. Rates, funding conditions, and the August 17–19 catalyst sequence provide the next confirmation points.
