Executive view

August 19 is centered on two scheduled U.S. catalysts: the Federal Reserve's minutes from the July 28–29 FOMC meeting at 2:00 p.m. ET and the EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report on its standard Wednesday cadence. The Fed minutes may affect expectations for the policy path and therefore U.S. rates, DXY and rate-sensitive risk assets. EIA inventory data can influence WTI and Brent through changes in the near-term supply-demand picture.

Key drivers (verified)

- Federal Reserve minutes: The Fed calendar schedules the July 28–29 FOMC minutes for August 19 at 2:00 p.m. ET. Source: fed-august-2026-calendar.

- July policy context: The July 29 FOMC statement maintained the federal funds target range at 3.50%–3.75%, with a 9–3 vote. Source: fed-july-2026-statement.

- EIA petroleum data: EIA's standard schedule releases the WPSR summary and core tables after 10:30 a.m. ET on Wednesdays, subject to listed holiday exceptions. Source: eia-wpsr-schedule.

- Labor calendar: BLS schedules July State Employment and Unemployment data for August 21 at 10:00 a.m. ET. Source: bls-august-2026-schedule.

Risks and conditional interpretations

- If the FOMC minutes are interpreted as emphasizing greater inflation or policy-tightening concern than markets had assumed, U.S. yields and the dollar could face upward pressure; a less restrictive interpretation could produce the opposite response. The relationship is regime-dependent.

- Larger-than-expected petroleum inventory changes may move WTI and Brent, but the effect on DXY, gold and broader risk sentiment can vary with the dominant macro driver.
