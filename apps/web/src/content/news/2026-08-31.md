Executive view

The primary scheduled USD catalyst in the next seven days is the September 4 Employment Situation for August. The sequence begins with July JOLTS on September 1, followed by the September 2 Beige Book and September 3 revised productivity data. The Federal Reserve calendar also lists Governor Michael Barr speaking on September 1 and Governor Christopher Waller speaking on September 3.

July PCE is not an August 31 event. BEA released the data on August 26: headline and core PCE prices each increased 0.2% month over month, while the 12-month rates were 3.7% and 3.3%, respectively. The next Personal Income and Outlays release is scheduled for September 30.

Treasury's current tentative schedule also requires precise language. August 31 includes 13-week and 26-week bill auctions, with settlement on September 3. Separately, several coupon securities and 30-year TIPS auctioned earlier in August settle on August 31. Auction date and settlement date should not be treated as interchangeable.

Key drivers (verified developments)

- Labor sequence: BLS schedules July JOLTS for September 1 at 10:00 a.m. ET and the August Employment Situation for September 4 at 8:30 a.m. ET. JOLTS may affect the labor-demand narrative before payrolls, but the Employment Situation remains the highest-impact scheduled release in this window. (Source: bls-september-schedule.)
- Growth and cost mix: BLS schedules revised second-quarter Productivity and Costs for September 3 at 8:30 a.m. ET. The interpretation depends on the source of the revision across output, hours, compensation, and unit labor costs. (Source: bls-september-schedule.)
- Fed communications: The official calendar lists Governor Barr on September 1, the Beige Book on September 2, and Governor Waller on September 3. These may affect policy expectations but are not substitutes for an FOMC decision. (Source: fed-september-calendar.)
- Recent inflation outcome: BEA released July Personal Income and Outlays on August 26. Headline and core PCE prices each rose 0.2% month over month; headline was 3.7% and core was 3.3% year over year. The next release is September 30. (Source: bea-personal-income-july.)
- Treasury liquidity calendar: The current tentative schedule lists 13-week and 26-week bill auctions on August 31 and separates those auctions from August 31 settlements for several coupon securities and 30-year TIPS. (Sources: treasury-q3-auction-schedule, treasurydirect-auction-timing, treasurydirect-announcements.)

Conditional cross-asset implications

- If JOLTS and payrolls jointly show firmer labor demand, front-end yields and the USD could rise as markets price a less accommodative Fed path. If both weaken broadly, yields and the USD could fall. Mixed details may produce a short-lived or two-way reaction.
- A productivity revision driven by stronger output could be interpreted differently from one driven mainly by fewer hours. Likewise, unit labor costs depend on both productivity and compensation, so the headline revision should not be read in isolation.
- More restrictive Fed communication could reinforce upward pressure on yields and the dollar; more cautious communication could do the opposite. The effect will depend on how remarks compare with incoming data and existing market pricing.
- Bill-auction demand and month-end settlement flows can affect short-end rates, dealer balance sheets, reserves, and repo conditions. The direction is not predetermined: strong demand and orderly funding can absorb supply, while weaker demand or balance-sheet pressure can tighten conditions.
- The July PCE outcome remains part of the inflation backdrop, but there is no new PCE release this week. Labor, productivity, Fed communication, and Treasury flows are the marginal scheduled drivers.
