## Executive view

**September 25 release review. Prepared September 25, 2026, 16:55 UTC.** This briefing uses official information available at preparation time. It is not a closing-market report or a live-price table. The regime label is an editorial description, not the measured Weekly Score.

Today's evidence spans three different layers: employee compensation, dealer financing and Federal Reserve balance-sheet liquidity. They should not be collapsed into one bullish or bearish dollar conclusion. The useful question is what each dataset adds to the labor, leverage and liquidity picture while keeping observation periods and definitions separate.

## 1. Employee benefits: compensation structure, not a payroll signal

The [BLS September 25 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ebs2.htm) reports that **72% of private-industry workers had access to retirement benefits in March 2026**, while **52% participated**. Defined contribution plans were available to **70%** of private-industry workers and defined benefit plans to **14%**.

Among state and local government workers, **92% had access to retirement benefits** and **81% participated**. BLS also reports that **71% of private-industry workers had access to medical care plans**, with **46% participating**.

**USD Impact interpretation:** Benefit access is part of total compensation, but it does not directly measure monthly hiring, wage growth, unemployment or inflation. Do not substitute this release for the forthcoming JOLTS, income or PCE data.

## 2. Dealer financing: broadly steady terms in the latest survey

The Federal Reserve's [September Senior Credit Officer Opinion Survey](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/scoos/scoos-202609.htm) covers changes in securities financing and OTC derivatives markets between **June and August 2026**. The Fed says **price and nonprice terms were basically unchanged on net across counterparty types**.

For hedge-fund clients, **88.2% of responding dealers said use of financial leverage remained basically unchanged** over the previous three months; 5.9% reported a somewhat higher use and 5.9% a somewhat lower use.

**USD Impact interpretation:** SCOOS describes dealer-reported financing conditions. It is not a live funding-spread series and it does not prove that market liquidity is easy or stressed. Use it alongside repo, Treasury-market, bank-balance-sheet and volatility evidence.

## 3. Federal Reserve balance sheet: reserves down, Treasury cash up

The latest [H.4.1 release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h41/Current/) shows **reserve balances with Federal Reserve Banks averaging $2.930 trillion** in the week ended **September 23**, down **$83.601 billion** from the prior week.

The **U.S. Treasury General Account averaged $977.084 billion**, up **$100.056 billion** from the previous week. Reserve Bank credit averaged **$6.702 trillion**.

**USD Impact interpretation:** A rise in Treasury cash can coincide with lower reserve balances, but the weekly accounting move is not by itself a trading signal. For the liquidity dial, compare reserve balances with Treasury cash management, repo conditions, bank funding and broader financial conditions.

## Key takeaway

Read the layer before reading the signal. Employee-benefit access describes compensation structure; SCOOS describes dealer-reported financing terms; H.4.1 records Federal Reserve balance-sheet conditions. None alone verifies the direction of DXY, rates, gold, Bitcoin or equities.
