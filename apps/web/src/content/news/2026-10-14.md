## What it is

A source-dated review of September consumer inflation, energy-price contributions and inflation-adjusted earnings, with the next scheduled BLS producer and import/export price publications identified separately.

## Why it matters

The BLS reports a **0.6% monthly rise in headline CPI** and **3.7% annual headline inflation**. Gasoline accounted for more than half of the monthly rise. At the same time, inflation-adjusted average hourly and weekly earnings both declined 0.5% for all private nonfarm employees in September. Price pressure and purchasing-power pressure can coexist; neither directly tells us where DXY or the ten-year TIPS yield traded today.

## What moves it

Gasoline and broader energy components are important to the headline CPI reading, while the core and shelter measures offer additional context. Higher-than-expected inflation *may* shift nominal or real rate expectations and dollar demand, but the actual market response depends on expectations, policy messaging, liquidity and global conditions. Household purchasing power can influence demand over time without mechanically driving one trading session.

## Common mistake

- Confusing monthly, seasonally adjusted CPI changes with the unadjusted 12-month rate.
- Treating the September gasoline **consumer-price index** rise as the October 14 spot or futures return for WTI, Brent or natural gas.
- Calling a scheduled October 15 PPI release an actual result.
- Using nominal Treasury yields in place of a measured ten-year TIPS **real** yield, or inferring DXY movement from inflation data alone.
