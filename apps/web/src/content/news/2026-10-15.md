## What it is

The September Producer Price Index for final demand, published October 15, with dated consumer inflation and real-pay context from October 14. Producer and consumer prices describe different stages and baskets, so their month-to-month percentage changes should not be added or confused.

## Why it matters

BLS reports **final-demand PPI +1.3% month over month** (seasonally adjusted) and **+6.3% over 12 months** (not seasonally adjusted). The final-demand energy component advanced **10.8%**, and gasoline prices at the producer stage rose **20.3%** in September. The prior day's consumer CPI was **+0.6% monthly** and **+3.7% annually**, while real hourly earnings for private nonfarm workers decreased **0.5% monthly**. Together these describe a dated inflation-and-purchasing-power picture, not real-time market moves.

## What moves it

Supply costs, energy markets, producer margins, service prices and demand conditions shape PPI. Potential dollar transmission depends on how these releases change expectations for future monetary policy, real yields, USD funding and risk appetite. WTI, Henry Hub and TTF also react to physical supply, storage, transport and international gas conditions. Equities, gold and Bitcoin have their own distinct drivers.

## Common mistake

Equating the September gasoline **producer-price index** rise with October 15 WTI or Brent price performance; confusing nominal yields with the ten-year TIPS real yield; treating PPI and CPI as the same measure; or presenting scheduled October 16 data as released.
