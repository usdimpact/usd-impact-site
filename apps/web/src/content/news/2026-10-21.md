Executive view

The BLS October 21 release reports median usual weekly earnings of $1,232 in the third quarter of 2026 for full-time wage and salary workers, up 1.5% from a year earlier. BLS compares that with a 3.5% rise in the CPI-U over the same year. The median is not seasonally adjusted; the separately seasonally adjusted median was $1,239, versus $1,258 in the previous quarter. These observations speak to pay and purchasing-power conditions but do not by themselves establish how DXY, yields, gold, or equities traded.

Yesterday's BLS state employment report kept the September national unemployment rate at 4.2%, with most states showing no statistically significant monthly rate or payroll change. EIA also announced that third-party petroleum spot-price observations would move from weekly WPSR tables to a daily publication cadence effective October 21.

Key drivers (verified official releases)

- Earnings, released October 21: Q3 median weekly pay was $1,232, up 1.5% year over year; CPI-U was up 3.5% over the same period. The wage median is not seasonally adjusted. The separate seasonally adjusted median was $1,239, down from $1,258 in Q2. Source: BLS Usual Weekly Earnings, Q3 2026.

- State labor conditions, released October 20: BLS reports September unemployment rates down in five states, little changed in 45 and Washington, D.C., and the national rate at 4.2%. Significant nonfarm payroll changes occurred in only three states: two increases and one decrease. Source: BLS State Employment and Unemployment, September 2026.

- Energy data access, beginning October 21: EIA's WPSR notice says third-party spot-price data would be published daily and Tables 11, 12 and 14 removed from the weekly report. This is a change in the distribution of data, not evidence of a new oil-price direction. Source: EIA WPSR notice.
