## Executive view

The evidence supports three separate observations: U.S. refineries have been running at unusually high rates this year; recent nominal weekly earnings growth lagged its CPI comparison; and the next major U.S. policy and labor-cost calendar includes the October 28 FOMC communications and October 30 Employment Cost Index.

## What the evidence means

**Energy supply context:** EIA's October 22 analysis says refinery utilization averaged more than 95% from May through September, the highest for those months since 1998. EIA linked the elevated runs to tight global product supplies and strong distillate margins. This can inform an energy-and-inflation discussion, but it is not a live WTI or Brent price signal.

**Purchasing-power context:** The BLS weekly-earnings release compares nominal pay growth with CPI-U over the same year-over-year period. That comparison is useful context for real purchasing power, but it should not be generalized into a current wage trend for every worker or household.

**Policy and data calendar:** The October 27-28 FOMC meeting is followed later in the week by the October 30 BLS Employment Cost Index for Q3 2026. These are scheduled events only. The actual statement, data and market response should be evaluated after publication.
