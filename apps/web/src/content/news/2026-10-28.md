## Executive view

The Federal Open Market Committee held the federal funds target range at **3.75%–4.00%** on October 28. The statement was approved by a **10–2 vote**. Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack dissented because they preferred a **25 basis point increase**.

The implementation note leaves the core operating settings unchanged: interest on reserve balances at **3.90%**, standing overnight repo at **4.0%**, overnight reverse repo at **3.75%** with a **$160 billion per-day per-counterparty limit**, and the primary credit rate at **4.0%**. These settings take effect October 29.

The statement says economic activity is expanding at a **strong pace**, inflation remains elevated, and accumulating more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to 2%. This edition reports those official facts without assigning a hawkish/dovish label or an unverified market reaction.

The next macro window remains concentrated. BEA identifies October 29 as the next release date for both the advance estimate of third-quarter GDP and September Personal Income and Outlays, including PCE inflation data. BLS lists the third-quarter Employment Cost Index for October 30.

## What changed today

**FOMC decision:** The Committee maintained the target range at **3.75%–4.00%**, unchanged from September. The vote changed from September's 12–0 decision to **10–2**, with Cook and Hammack preferring to raise the range by 25 basis points.

**Implementation:** The Federal Reserve left IORB at **3.90%**, the standing overnight repo rate at **4.0%**, the ON RRP offering rate at **3.75%** with the **$160 billion daily per-counterparty limit**, and the primary credit rate at **4.0%**. The Desk directive continues to include Treasury-bill purchases, and if needed other Treasury securities with remaining maturities of three years or less, to maintain ample reserves, along with Treasury rollovers and reinvestment of agency principal into Treasury bills.

**Statement language:** Compared with September, the activity description moved from a **solid pace** to a **strong pace**. Inflation is still described as elevated. The October statement says the accumulation of more information will help determine the appropriate policy stance. This is a textual comparison of official statements, not a prediction of the next policy move.

**Regional labor data:** BLS separately reported that September unemployment rates were lower than a year earlier in 226 of 387 metropolitan areas, higher in 138 and unchanged in 23. Nonfarm employment increased over the year in 11 metropolitan areas, decreased in 3 and was essentially unchanged in 373.
