## Executive view

**The new October 30 release is BLS's third-quarter Employment Cost Index (ECI).** Civilian-worker total compensation costs increased **0.8% quarter over quarter, seasonally adjusted**, compared with **0.9% in Q2**. Q3 wages and salaries increased **0.7%** and benefit costs **0.9%** over the quarter, also seasonally adjusted. Over 12 months, compensation costs were **3.4% higher, not seasonally adjusted**, with wages **3.1%** higher and benefits **3.9%** higher. These are official published observations, not estimated market surprises.

The prior day's BEA releases reported **3.4% annualized Q3 real GDP growth** and September headline PCE inflation of **3.6% year over year** (core **3.1%**). The October 28 Fed decision kept the target policy range at **3.75%–4.00%**. Taken together, these sources establish a growth, inflation and labor-cost backdrop; they do **not** establish a realized move in USD, DXY, U.S. yields or any other asset.

## What changed

**Labor costs — BLS ECI, October 30:** Q3 civilian-worker compensation rose **0.8%** over three months (seasonally adjusted), versus **0.9%** in Q2. The Q3 breakdown is wages **+0.7%** and benefits **+0.9%** quarterly. On the **12-month, not-seasonally-adjusted** basis, compensation is **+3.4%**, wages **+3.1%**, benefits **+3.9%**. Do not confuse the 0.8% quarterly rate with a 12-month rate; do not characterize a change versus consensus without a cited expectation benchmark.

**Growth — BEA Q3 GDP, October 29:** The Q3 advance estimate puts real GDP growth at **3.4% at a seasonally adjusted annual rate**, up from **2.2% in Q2**. This is a *quarterly annualized real growth rate*, not a year-over-year figure.

**Inflation — BEA September PCE, October 29:** The PCE **price index** rose **0.5% month over month** and **3.6% year over year**. The index excluding food and energy rose **0.3% monthly** and **3.1% annually**. Nominal PCE *spending* increased **0.5% monthly**, which is a different metric. Nor should the GDP report's annualized quarterly PCE inflation measures be interchanged with September's monthly or 12-month inflation rates.

**Policy — Federal Reserve, October 28:** The FOMC maintained the federal funds target range at **3.75%–4.00%**, with a **10–2** vote. Two policymakers preferred a 25-basis-point increase; the implementation note maintained interest on reserve balances at **3.90%**, effective October 29. These are confirmed policy facts, not evidence of an October 30 market response.
