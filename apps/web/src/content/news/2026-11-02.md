## What it is and what changed

**U.S. labor costs:** The Employment Cost Index is a measure of employer compensation costs. Third-quarter civilian compensation rose 0.8% over the previous quarter; wages rose 0.7% and benefits rose 0.9%, on seasonally adjusted bases. The 3.4% 12-month compensation increase is not seasonally adjusted. Both readings describe the period ending in September, not November 2.

**U.S. growth:** BEA's third-quarter real GDP advance estimate was 3.4% at a seasonally adjusted annual rate, up from 2.2% in the second quarter. This is the latest cited quarterly estimate, not a forecast of fourth-quarter GDP or a measurement of year-over-year growth.

**New York Fed operations:** The New York Fed said its trading desk plans a November 3 small-value nominal Treasury sale for operational testing, up to $50 million face value in a specified maturity range. The scheduled exercise should not be confused with a new Fed rate decision, large-scale asset sale program or verified change in liquidity conditions. The announced plan and later executed results are separate facts.

**LNG and Latin America:** EIA reported that Colombia's average annual marketed natural-gas production declined from 1,070 MMcf/d in 2022 to 772 MMcf/d in 2025. The agency describes greater reliance on LNG imports and identifies drought as a conditional demand factor for power generation. This does not establish an immediate Henry Hub or TTF move.

## Why this matters for the dollar and cross assets

The key educational transmission chain is **growth and compensation data -> expected policy path -> nominal and real yields -> foreign exchange and risk-sensitive assets**. Each link depends on expectations, positioning, and independently observed prices. A higher growth estimate or a lower quarterly compensation reading is not, on its own, a trading signal.

For **USD and DXY**, verify which dollar index and timestamp are being discussed; do not treat them as interchangeable. For **gold and U.S. equity indices**, real rates, discount rates and risk appetite may matter more than a single data point. For **Bitcoin**, liquidity and market positioning can alter any cross-asset relation. For **LNG, Henry Hub and TTF**, regional supply, contracting, shipping and weather can dominate a broad-dollar interpretation. None of these channels implies a guaranteed price direction.
