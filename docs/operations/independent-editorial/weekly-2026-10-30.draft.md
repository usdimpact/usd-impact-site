## Executive summary

The week’s verified USD Impact Dailies documented a sequence of separate events: the October 27–28 FOMC meeting was previewed before the announcement; the Federal Reserve issued its decision on October 28; BEA published growth and inflation statistics on October 29; and BLS published the third-quarter Employment Cost Index on October 30. They do not independently establish what DXY, Treasury yields, oil, natural gas, gold, Bitcoin or U.S. stocks actually did in response.

## What happened

**October 26–27 — upcoming, not yet decided.** The first two [Dailies](https://www.usd-impact.com/news/2026-10-26/) and [October 27 edition](https://www.usd-impact.com/news/2026-10-27/) cited the [official Fed calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2026-october.htm). These were schedule observations. Count them as calendar context, not two policy actions.

**October 28 — Fed decision.** The [official FOMC statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm), referenced by the [October 28 Daily](https://www.usd-impact.com/news/2026-10-28/), says the target range remained 3.75%–4.00% by a 10–2 vote, with two members preferring a 25-basis-point increase. This is a dated policy decision, not evidence of a same-day realized dollar movement.

**October 29 — economic activity and price indexes.** The [BEA Q3 GDP advance release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-3rd-quarter-2026) recorded real GDP growth of 3.4% at a seasonally adjusted annual rate, compared with Q2’s 2.2%. The separate [BEA September Personal Income and Outlays release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-september-2026) reported headline PCE inflation at 3.6% year over year and core PCE at 3.1% year over year. GDP growth, monthly inflation, year-over-year inflation and spending are different measurements. These claims are anchored to the already [published October 30 Daily](https://www.usd-impact.com/news/2026-10-30/), not an assumption that an early October 29 generatedAt anticipated later issuer releases.

**October 30 — labor costs.** The dated [BLS Employment Cost Index release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/eci_10302026.htm) reported Q3 civilian compensation growth of 0.8% quarter over quarter, seasonally adjusted, versus 0.9% in Q2, and 3.4% over 12 months, not seasonally adjusted. The [October 30 published Daily](https://www.usd-impact.com/news/2026-10-30/) retained its earlier 09:34 UTC generatedAt and received a separately owner-reviewed post-12:30 UTC editorial revision in [PR 1009](https://github.com/usdimpact/usd-impact-site/pull/1009#issuecomment-6525502611). This draft does not misstate when the BLS numbers first became available.

## Why it matters for the USD framework

Central-bank policy can affect expected short-term interest rates. Inflation and labor-cost observations can alter how markets assess that policy path; economic growth affects expectations for demand. In turn, changes in rates, real yields, relative returns and risk appetite may transmit into currency and cross-asset markets. Those are **conditional mechanisms**; official calendar entries and economic releases are not measurements of contemporaneous asset-price movements.

The weekly synthesis must never claim a measured USD Impact Score or invent a stronger/weaker-dollar regime. It contains no price-feed downloads, DXY/WTI/VIX/Gold/BTC close-to-close series, component z-scores, momentum or unlicensed data redistribution.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not double-count the calendar preview and FOMC outcome. Do not treat Q3 annualized GDP growth as year-over-year GDP or compare the ECI quarterly seasonally adjusted increase directly with its 12-month unadjusted change. Do not infer a market surprise without a properly documented forecast comparison. Do not claim asset returns or the direction of the U.S. dollar without independently dated, rights-cleared market evidence.

## What to watch next

Keep the upcoming official publication calendar separate from the outcomes eventually released. Track any future policy, inflation and labor-market releases by their issue date, reference period, measurement units and provenance. Reassess the educational transmission hypotheses only when separate, licensed or independently authorized market observations can support a realized-move claim.
