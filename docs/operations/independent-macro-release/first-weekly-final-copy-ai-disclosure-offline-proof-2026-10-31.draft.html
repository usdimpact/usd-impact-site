01 / Executive summary

Four releases, one important distinction

The FOMC held its target range at 3.75%–4.00% on October 28. A day later, the Bureau of Economic Analysis released third-quarter GDP and September personal income and outlays data. On October 30, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported third-quarter employment costs.

These official observations help frame how monetary policy, economic activity and inflation can influence expectations for the U.S. dollar. They do not establish whether DXY, Treasury yields, WTI, gas, gold, Bitcoin or U.S. equity indexes actually rose or fell during those releases.

02 / Verified chronology

What happened, in order

October 26–27 · Before the decision

The Fed meeting was scheduled, not decided

Early-week Daily editions documented the October 27–28 FOMC meeting on the official calendar. A calendar entry should never be counted as a policy outcome.

Federal Reserve Board event calendar
October 28 · Policy decision

The target range stayed unchanged

The FOMC maintained the federal funds target range at 3.75%–4.00%. The statement recorded a 10–2 vote, with two participants favoring a 25-basis-point increase. That describes the decision; it does not demonstrate a contemporaneous dollar-price reaction.

October 29 · Economic growth

Q3 real GDP advanced at a 3.4% annualized pace

BEA’s advance estimate reported 3.4% seasonally adjusted annualized real GDP growth in the third quarter, following 2.2% in the second. Quarterly annualized growth is not the same measurement as year-over-year growth.

BEA GDP advance release · 8:30 a.m. EDT issuer-stated release time
October 29 · Inflation

Headline and core PCE remained distinct

The September release reported 3.6% headline PCE inflation and 3.1% core PCE inflation over twelve months. These are year-over-year rates, not monthly changes or direct measurements of market expectations.

BEA Personal Income and Outlays release · 8:30 a.m. EDT
October 30 · Employment costs

Employment costs rose 0.8% during Q3

BLS reported a 0.8% seasonally adjusted quarter-over-quarter increase in civilian compensation, compared with 0.9% in the preceding quarter. The separate twelve-month increase was 3.4% on a not-seasonally-adjusted basis.

03 / Transmission framework

Why the releases matter for the dollar

Official facts are inputs to analysis, not a ready-made currency signal. The economic transmission chain is conditional: information changes expectations; expectations can affect rates, risk appetite and relative asset returns; market prices then reflect many simultaneous influences.

01 / POLICYExpected short rates

FOMC decisions can shift the perceived path of future interest rates.

02 / ACTIVITYGrowth and demand

GDP data can change assessments of economic resilience and demand.

03 / PRICESInflation and labor costs

PCE and ECI can shape the outlook for inflation persistence.

None of these channels establishes the direction, magnitude or timing of an actual move in the dollar, oil, natural gas, gold, Bitcoin or U.S. indexes. A realized-move claim requires independently verified, rights-cleared market observations not included in this preview.

04 / Interpretation

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not double-count. The October 26–27 meeting previews and October 28 policy decision are different stages of one event.

Do not mix measurement units. GDP growth is presented as an annualized quarterly pace; PCE uses twelve-month inflation rates; ECI includes both quarterly seasonally adjusted and annual not-seasonally-adjusted readings.

Do not invent the market reaction. A dated release does not establish a surprise relative to forecasts, nor can it prove what any asset price did on that day.

05 / What to watch

Questions for the next release cycle

Track the next official calendar, distinguish scheduled announcements from published outcomes, and check the reference period and units of every observation. When new facts appear, review their potential policy implications before drawing conditional cross-asset connections.

The method is straightforward: verify the source, date the evidence, distinguish mechanism from observed price movement, and record what remains unknown.
