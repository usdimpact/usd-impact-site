## Slot 4 — IG-006 — DXY in 30 Seconds

### Caption

DXY is useful, but it is not the whole global dollar system.

It tracks the U.S. dollar against a specific basket of major currencies. That makes it a practical exchange-rate signal.

But global dollar funding also runs through loans, deposits, bonds, FX swaps, repo, collateral and cross-border contracts.

Use DXY for the question it answers. Do not ask it to represent the entire system.

**CTA:** Learn the framework → Start Here

Educational only.

## Slot 5 — IG-011 — Dollar vs Gold

### Caption

A stronger dollar can create pressure for gold.

That does **not** create a permanent rule.

Real yields, risk demand, inflation expectations, positioning and other gold-specific forces can reinforce or offset the dollar channel.

The useful question is not “Does the dollar always move gold?”

It is: **Which driver is dominant in this move?**

## Slot 6 — IG-021 — Repo in Plain English

### Caption

Repo is short-term secured funding.

One party receives cash and provides securities as collateral, with the transaction later reversed under agreed terms.

Why does that matter?

Because repo is part of the plumbing that helps finance securities and support market liquidity.

A repo move is not automatically a trading signal. It is one funding channel to read inside the broader system.

**CTA:** Learn the Dollar Transmission Chain → USD Impact

Educational only.

### Fallback caption — IG-007 Real Yields in 30 Seconds

A nominal yield does not tell you the whole rate story.

A simple conceptual relationship is:

**Real yield = nominal yield − expected inflation**

Real yields help frame inflation-adjusted financing and discount-rate pressure.

They can matter for assets such as gold and long-duration equities, but the relationship is not mechanical.

Use real yields as one important dial, not the entire explanation.
