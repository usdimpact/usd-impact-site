## How the financial incident generation advances (verified source, not an owner policy)

In the original, still-uninstalled Research P1 lifecycle migration, \`research_lifecycle_private.record_post_finalization_billing_incident\` inserts an \`open\` row with \`first_observed_at=last_observed_at=v_now\` and \`observation_count=1\` (default). For a later incident on that same account, its \`ON CONFLICT (account_id) DO UPDATE\` refreshes \`last_observed_at=v_now\`, increments \`observation_count=i.observation_count+1\`, returns \`status='open'\`, retains/merges reason codes, and clears prior resolution references and \`resolved_at\`. A previous adjudication therefore cannot be treated as covering later evidence. The timestamp comes from \`clock_timestamp()\`; the counter provides a separate monotonically advancing dimension even when two observations have close timestamps.

A financial **generation** should be identified as the *pair* \`(last_observed_at, observation_count)\` for a given account and supervised case. #1232's \`a_strict_financial_reconciliations\` table uses \`unique(account_id, incident_last_observed_at, incident_observation_count)\`, **but its owner function invokes the legacy resolver before appending that ledger row**, and no existing trigger requires the ledger for a *direct* resolver call. Thus generation-aware *logging* alone does not impose generation-aware *authorization*.

#1230's reviewed B1 instead uses an \`a_strict_owner_resolutions\` decision with \`unique(account_id,case_ref,incident_last_observed_at)\` and a \`BEFORE UPDATE\` guard when a matching A-strict case exists. It does not also bind \`observation_count\`, and the case-scoped \`if found and not exists(...)\` guard does not refuse an owner legacy resolver call for an account with no case. **Neither design is independently sufficient.**
